## Page 2: Sometimes You Don't Know What You're Looking For — Yet

- This kind of searching is called **exploratory search** (Hoeber 2025)
- You don't have one clear question — you're still figuring out what you even want to know
- So it takes more than one search, one page, one sitting — real searchers often need 3 or more sessions to finish a task like this (Li et al. 2020)

---

## Page 3: And It Gets Overwhelming Fast

- Lots of pages, lots of tabs, lots of half-read things
- Easy to lose track of what you've already seen (Gomes et al. 2022)
- Your brain is holding all of it "in the air" at once, while still searching

---

## Page 4: Now Add Low Working Memory to That

- Working memory = what your brain holds "in the air" while you think
- When it's limited, people forget what they already searched, and what they already found (Choi and Arguello 2025)
- This hits harder for people with **dyslexia**, which research links to working-memory deficits specifically (Choi and Arguello 2025)
- It also hits harder for people with **ADHD** (Zhu et al. 2026) — and the two often overlap in the same person (Zhu et al. 2026)

---

## Page 5: Researchers Already Tried to Help

- **Folders & notes** — drag what you find into folders, label it, organize it (Crescenzi et al. 2021 — "OrgBox")
- **Timelines** — an interactive timeline of your past search history, so you can see where you left off (Hoeber, Islam, et al. 2024)
- **Visual keyword maps** — lines drawn between results that share concepts, so you can see how what you found connects (Hoeber and Shukla 2022)
- These genuinely helped — but one thing kept slipping through

---

## Page 6: The Thing That's Still Missing: "Wait — Where Was I?"

- That moment is called **metacognition** — thinking about your own thinking
- Before you dive back in, you need to plan, monitor, and judge your own progress (Crescenzi et al. 2021)
- Folders and maps show you *what* you did — not *what you were thinking*

---

## Page 7: This Moment Matters 3 Times

- **Before** you search — what am I even trying to do?
- **During** — am I on track, or should I pivot?
- **After a break** — getting back into the headspace of the task, called **reacquaintance** (Li, Capra, and Zhang 2020)
- Right now, that "getting back into it" part is left entirely up to you

---

## Page 8: Think of a Buddy Who Helps You Remember

- Ever get interrupted mid-sentence, then a friend says "you were talking about X, then Y..." — and it comes back to you?
- One cue leads to the next, and your train of thought returns
- This works because the *right cues* bring back the *right memory* (Tulving and Thomson 1973)

---

## Page 9: But That Kind of Buddy Doesn't Scale

- Not everyone has someone who can do that, every time, for every search
- Especially for the millions of people with dyslexia or low working memory
- A one-on-one human helper for everyone isn't realistic

---

## Page 10: AI Can Be That Buddy

- Organize what was found, remind you what happened, even suggest a next step
- Some AI tools already do pieces of this: prompting you to set goals and check your progress (Zhu et al. 2026), suggesting helpful follow-up questions (Yang et al. 2025), or grouping what you've found into themes (Katz et al. 2024)
- Sometimes faster and more consistent than a person could be, and always available

---

## Page 11: But the Buddy Never Decides for You

- A real buddy jogs your memory — they don't make the decision for you
- The AI here should work the exact same way: it helps you remember, **you** choose what's next (Hoeber, CHIIR '25)
- Too much automation risks you checking out entirely (Risko and Gilbert 2016) — that's a risk we design against, not ignore

---

## Page 12: That Kind of Help Has a Name: Scaffolding

- Like scaffolding on a building — temporary support that helps you build, without building it for you
- Here, it's always there when you need it, and steps back when you don't
- It supports your thinking — it doesn't replace it

---

## Page 13: So, Here's What We're Proposing

- An **AI-assisted metacognitive scaffold** — an AI memory helper for search
- Helps you plan before, stay oriented during, and reacquaint after a break
- AI helps you remember and re-orient — you always make the final call

---

## Page 14: Who This Helps

- Anyone short on time who searches across multiple sessions
- Especially people with **low working memory** or **dyslexia** — same root struggle: losing the thread (Choi and Arguello 2025)
- Help one group well, and most people benefit too
